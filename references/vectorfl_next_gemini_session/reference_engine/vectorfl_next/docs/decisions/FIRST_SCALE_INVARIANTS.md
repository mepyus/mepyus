# First-Scale Invariants

## Purpose

This document does **not** finalize the whole ontology of the future space.

It only locks the minimum invariants that must keep holding after first-scale expansion.

If these break, the current engine risks collapsing back into early-closing relation logic.

## Locked Invariants

### 1. Space stays primary

- `material -> trace -> point_seed -> space_cell -> local_space -> bridge_trace` remains the core formation order.
- Relation may emerge only after space has formed.
- Bridge cannot become the primary center of growth.

### 2. Merge remains prohibited

- `bridge_trace` is exposure, not collapse.
- Separate local spaces may become adjacent, resonant, or terrain-shared.
- They may not be flattened into one absolute space by bridge logic.

### 3. Append-only history remains mandatory

- Runtime artifacts are not rewritten into a clean final story.
- Reruns, mismatches, branch residue, and softened runtime differences remain observable process traces.
- Lived runtime shape may differ from isolated replay shape, and that difference must be preservable.

### 4. Raw material remains preservable

- `material` remains the first-class incoming unit.
- Raw payload is not replaced by summary-first abstractions.
- Logs, reports, code residue, failed experiments, reading notes, and weak traces can all enter as material.

### 5. Quiet space is valid space

- A local space does not need immediate bridge exposure to count as valid formation.
- Sparse, reflux, non-purpose, failed, and unknown materials may remain as quiet local spaces.
- Visibility is not the only test of presence.

### 6. Scale-up may widen without bridge growth

- Scale growth is allowed to happen by increasing local spaces and terrain components alone.
- Bridge count is not a required proxy for successful expansion.
- Quiet-side widening is a valid scale event.

### 7. Time stays evidence-first

- Time is still treated as maturation evidence, not a hard admission gate.
- Boundary durability remains the main maturity signal for local space.
- Bridge persistence may age, but time must not become an early cut/lock mechanism.

### 8. Reread stays secondary

- Observer, report, audit, and reread layers are descriptive only.
- They may help read climate, rhythm, memory, retention, and forgetting.
- They may not replace or compress the underlying runtime into a thinner story.

### 9. Sparse-presence review stays parked

- Sparse persistence is important, but it is still too early to judge it as complete.
- The current runtime must keep growing before sparse-presence review becomes decisive.
- The checkpoint remains active, but deferred.

### 10. Scale tests must preserve multi-speed formation

- The engine must be allowed to host fast relation-heavy terrain and slow quiet terrain at the same time.
- If scale-up begins collapsing everything into what becomes visible fastest, the current physics has failed.

## What Is Intentionally Not Locked Yet

- final meaning of point/cluster-like large-scale emergence
- final sparse-presence verdict
- final graph-view or human viewer representation
- final extraction pipeline behavior
- final large-scale runtime density threshold

## Current Reading

After first-scale expansion, the engine has already shown:

- resonant terrain can persist
- weak bridge exposure can accumulate without merge
- quiet local spaces can widen the runtime without increasing bridge count
- sparse, reflux, reading-note, failed, unknown, and process-residue materials can all form local spaces

That is enough to lock these invariants.

It is not enough to finalize the whole future space.
