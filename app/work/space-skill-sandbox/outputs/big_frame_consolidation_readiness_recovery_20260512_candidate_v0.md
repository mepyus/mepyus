## 8. Tension / Conflict Recovery

### 8.1 Depth vs Thin Surface

Why it exists:
  The reservoir needs depth, but re-entry needs smallness.

What it protects:
  original-space richness and usable next-session focus.

What it risks:
  hiding necessary warnings or forcing broad reread.

How to keep it as WATCH rather than fix it prematurely:
  keep thin surface provisional and preserve source references for deeper reads.

### 8.2 Caution vs Speed

Why it exists:
  promotion caution prevents false authority but can slow useful action.

What it protects:
  user judgment and authority separation.

What it risks:
  decision delay and excessive skepticism.

How to keep it as WATCH rather than fix it prematurely:
  use proportional caution based on risk and keep HOLD/WATCH real.

### 8.3 Recovery vs Ceremony Bloat

Why it exists:
  recovery preserves judgment, but repeated forms can become ritual.

What it protects:
  return usefulness and provenance.

What it risks:
  large recovery records increasing re-entry cost.

How to keep it as WATCH rather than fix it prematurely:
  recover usable judgment, not every detail.

### 8.4 Sandbox Freedom vs Sandbox Output Fake Authority

Why it exists:
  sandbox makes experiments safe, but formatted results look official.

What it protects:
  experimentation and reservoir integrity.

What it risks:
  trial outputs becoming memory, baseline, or product architecture.

How to keep it as WATCH rather than fix it prematurely:
  keep sandbox outputs return-bound and do-not-promote labeled.

### 8.5 Active Surface Usefulness vs Current-position Confusion

Why it exists:
  active surface helps re-entry, but it can look like an anchor.

What it protects:
  continuity and selection cost.

What it risks:
  current-position drift or automatic next task behavior.

How to keep it as WATCH rather than fix it prematurely:
  keep active surface explicitly non-current-position.

### 8.6 ChatGPT Supervisor Role vs User Authority

Why it exists:
  ChatGPT helps judge returns, but user remains final decision point.

What it protects:
  recovery speed and user intent.

What it risks:
  supervisor wording becoming hidden authority.

How to keep it as WATCH rather than fix it prematurely:
  label supervisor verdicts as candidate and user-mediated.

### 8.7 Consolidation vs Premature Workflow

Why it exists:
  the six layers can now be read together, but consolidation can harden too quickly.

What it protects:
  big-frame readability.

What it risks:
  workflow, registry, baseline, or product architecture drift.

How to keep it as WATCH rather than fix it prematurely:
  package readiness first; defer Big Frame Candidate Map creation.
