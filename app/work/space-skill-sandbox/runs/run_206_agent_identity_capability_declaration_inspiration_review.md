## 2. What the Idea Means

Agent identity / capability declaration means each worker should make clear:

- who or what it is
- what role it is currently playing
- what it can do
- what it cannot do
- what authority it does not have

In simple terms:

```text
Say who is acting.
Say what the actor can do.
Say what the actor is allowed to do in this task.
Say what the actor must not decide.
```

Capability is not permission.

## 3. Closest Concepts in Our Space

- User as final decision gate: final purpose, approval, and direction remain with the User
- ChatGPT as direction / validation / philosophy-check surface: checks fit and boundary drift
- Codex as structuring / packaging / documentation / bounded reviewer: prepares scope and records, but does not self-authorize implementation
- Gemini as execution / observation / read-heavy runner: executes or observes only when separately approved
- CLI as optional execution tool: availability does not imply use
- worker role boundary: separates validation, structuring, execution, and final control
- Codex/Gemini handoff records: preserve who did what and under which boundary
- no autonomous authority: worker confidence does not create approval
- no tool capability = permission: a tool can exist without being authorized for the task

No new folders, schemas, permission tables, or automation are proposed.

- do not treat capability declaration as permission grant
- do not let Codex self-assign broader authority
- do not let Gemini become verifier or truth source
- do not let CLI availability imply execution approval
- do not turn worker identity into fixed bureaucracy
- do not make every small action require role paperwork
- do not use capability declaration as automation/router basis
