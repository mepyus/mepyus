## 6. Key Findings

1. Operating 15 should remain audit lenses, not law, workflow, schema, ontology, or task queue.
2. Principles explain why to look a certain way; rules constrain what may or may not happen inside a declared scope.
3. Sandbox rules can be binding inside sandbox scope, but sandbox-local language must not become source-space law.
4. Candidate references can support reread and comparison, but they cannot approve movement, adoption, implementation, or package transition.
5. Watch items preserve risk; they are not hard prohibitions unless the User explicitly promotes them into rules.
6. Run records preserve trace and judgment path; they do not grant authority, create ledgers, or approve packages.
7. Current-position entries and next-chat summaries reduce re-entry cost, but they are not registry, index, task queue, or approval surface.
8. The four-line card remains useful as a user-facing aid only; repeated usefulness must not harden into protocol.
