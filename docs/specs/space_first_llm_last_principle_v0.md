# space first llm last principle v0

## verdict

- chronicle output must not be template-first and llm-filled
- the space must mature first
- the llm must come last as a writer, not first as the source of meaning

## why this principle is needed

The current risk is clear.

If we define a nice chronicle format first and let an llm fill it, then:

- the output may look rich
- but it may not be the space’s result
- it becomes unclear whether the meaning came from:
  - actual internal traces and line movement
  - or from llm fluency

At that point, the system drifts toward:

- “space as prompt material”

rather than:

- “space as the engine of meaning maturation”

## core rule

The order must be:

1. internal material accumulation
2. trace recovery
3. line survival / modification / merge visibility
4. hub meaning maturation in the space
5. human-language chronicle writing

The order must not be:

1. desired chronicle shape
2. llm completion
3. post-hoc attachment of space references

## what counts as good output

Good output means:

- the relevant internal materials are actually recovered
- the traced connections are real
- repeated lines are actually visible
- modification / merge / tension is supported by the recovered record
- the final human-language chronicle reads like a consequence of the space

In this case, the llm acts as:

- recorder
- arranger
- human-language writer

not as:

- primary source of conceptual meaning

## what counts as bad output

Bad output means:

- the chronicle shape looks convincing
- but the line trace is thin or absent
- the concept movement is mostly inferred by style
- the structure is filled because the template expects it
- the space appears to speak, but the llm is actually inventing the continuity

In this case, the result is not “space-first”.
It is:

- llm-first
- space-themed prose

## relation to templates

Templates may still be used, but only as loose scaffolds.

Template rules:

- a template must not force missing sections to be fabricated
- a concept may have strong incoming lines but weak merge lines
- a concept may have tension without a neat chronicle arc
- if the trace is incomplete, the output must remain incomplete

So:

- template is scaffold
- template is not ontology
- template is not answer key

## human-language chronicle rule

A human-language chronicle is legitimate only when:

- the space has already done enough maturation work
- line movement is visible enough to support narration

Therefore:

- the chronicle is a derivative layer
- not the primary layer

The chronicle should be thought of as:

- the writing down of matured space meaning

not:

- the manufacture of meaning by wording

## operator / supervisor reading rule

When reading a chronicle, the supervisor should always be able to ask:

- what internal materials support this?
- what line movement supports this?
- where was this concept revised?
- what merge or tension actually happened?

If those questions cannot be answered, then the output is too llm-first.

## non-goals

- no new runtime behavior in this note
- no ban on llm use
- no denial of human-language synthesis
- no claim that every concept is already mature enough for chronicle output

## current conclusion

- the space must come first
- llm must come last
- chronicle output is valid only when it is the written surface of an already maturing trace, not a polished substitute for one

## one-line principle

공간이 먼저 익고, LLM은 나중에 그것을 적어야 한다.  
반대로 되면 공간은 엔진이 아니라 소재가 된다.
