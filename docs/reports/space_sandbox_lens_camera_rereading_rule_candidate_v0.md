This file is not a baseline, official workflow, merge plan, structural redesign spec, or source-space law. It records a current operating rule candidate so User, ChatGPT, Codex, and Gemini can stay aligned while rereading the main space and sandbox.

## 2. Why this rule exists

The space has grown large and contains many kinds of Markdown memory, including:

- reports
- closeouts
- thought assets
- watch items
- candidates
- baselines
- external lens reinterpretations
- sandbox records
- runtime traces
- calibration material
- user/engine translation material

Because of this, the space cannot be read through one lens only. A single lens may reveal one layer of meaning while hiding context, process, risk, or reuse boundaries.

## 3. Core distinction: Space vs Sandbox

### Space

The main space is the accumulated memory layer.

It contains:

- philosophy
- operating memory
- decision traces
- failures and corrections
- candidate/watch/baseline records
- external material reinterpretations
- user-facing and engine-facing language
- process residue

### Sandbox

The sandbox is not the space itself.

It is a testing environment for:

- reading methods
- lens behavior
- camera behavior
- Gemini/Codex performance
- tiny utility behavior
- bounded package review
- calibration and overreach detection

Sandbox tools are helper candidates, not official workflows.

## 4. Lens and camera rule

- Lens = what perspective we apply to see meaning.
- Camera = how we observe, capture, and record what the lens reveals.

Examples of lenses:

- thought asset lens
- boundary lens
- provenance lens
- user-language lens
- engine-flow lens
- failure/recovery lens
- promotion-boundary lens
- residue lens
- external-material lens
- sandbox lens
- operating-principle lens

Rule: do not define the whole space from one lens. Each lens reveals some meaning and may distort other meaning.

## 5. Reading frame

When reading internal documents, separate:

- information
- context
- flow
- process
- result
- native lens
- additional lens readings
- dangerous assumption
- safe reframe
- what not to promote
- what needs human verification

This frame is a reading aid, not a mandate to restructure files.

## 7. Asset re-evaluation rule

Asset re-evaluation is not cleanup.

It asks:

- What kind of asset is this?
- What context produced it?
- What lens makes it useful?
- Is it a report, thought asset, candidate, watch item, baseline, trace, calibration material, or local residue?
- What should be reused?
- What should remain local/context-bound?
- What should not be promoted?
- What future evidence would change its status?

Do not classify assets as simply dead/alive. Do not delete, move, rename, or promote based on this rule.

## 8. Philosophy preservation / direction adjustment

The core philosophy remains stable:

- human judgment remains primary
- AI tools have roles, not authority
- failure and correction are memory assets
- candidate/watch/baseline must remain distinct
- automation must not outrun evidence
- external material is a lens, not a law
- sandbox is a test space, not the main space
- records matter because they preserve why decisions were made

Direction may be adjusted when repeated evidence shows that a different operating route is more useful.

## 9. Role operation rule

- User sets purpose and approves direction.
- ChatGPT designs direction, lens, validation architecture, and operating model.
- Codex structures the design into packages, internal files, review formats, and execution boundaries.
- Gemini executes read-heavy runs, observes, and returns evidence/material.
- ChatGPT reviews Gemini/Codex outputs and detects overreach.
- User remains final controller.

Codex must not act as final judge. Gemini must not turn read-heavy observation into implementation. ChatGPT direction should remain validation-oriented unless the User explicitly changes the mode.

## 10. Gemini calibration instruction

Gemini should read this rule candidate before the next space-reading run.

Gemini should remember:

- Do not define the whole space from one cluster.
- Do not treat reports as rules.
- Do not treat usage notes as official workflow.
- Do not treat candidate as baseline.
- Do not treat watch item as task.
- Do not treat sandbox helper as main program asset.
- Do not treat confidence as truth.
- Do not turn reading into implementation.
- Always separate evidence from interpretation.
- Always state what should not be inferred.

Before executing a new analysis, Gemini should produce a short calibration note answering:

1. What is the difference between space and sandbox?
2. What is a lens?
3. What is a camera?
4. Why should one lens not define the whole space?
5. How should the sandbox 15 principles be used?
6. What must Gemini avoid promoting?
7. What evidence/interpretation separation must Gemini preserve?

## 12. Current recommended loop

1. Read main space cluster.
2. Identify information/context/flow/process/result/lens.
3. Test lens/camera behavior in sandbox only if needed.
4. Reread sandbox result through the 15 operating principles.
5. Re-evaluate assets as candidate/watch/baseline/thought asset/local residue.
6. Preserve philosophy.
7. Adjust direction only with repeated evidence.
8. Return to User/ChatGPT for design-level judgment.
