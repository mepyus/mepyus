## 3. External Case Harvest

### A. Ambiguity in Design / Sensemaking
- **Key Concepts:** Productive Ambiguity (Monika Büscher), Sensemaking (Karl Weick).
- **External View:** Ambiguity is a *resource* that allows a system to remain flexible until the final requirements are clear.
- **Borrowable Asset:** **"Ambiguity Preservation as a Safety Protocol."** Keep early residues in a "provisional/fluid" state until they cross the "Novelty/Recurrence" gate.

### B. Boundary Objects (Star & Griesemer)
- **Key Concepts:** Objects that are "plastic enough to adapt" but "robust enough to maintain identity."
- **External View:** Knowledge coordination works by creating objects that mean different things to different communities (e.g., a "Map").
- **Borrowable Asset:** **"Lens-Specific Projections."** A Stage 0 residue shouldn't be *one* thing, but a collection of layer-specific interpretations.

### C. Emergent/Lightweight Ontology
- **Key Concepts:** Folksonomy, Gradual Formalization (Shipman & Marshall).
- **External View:** Start with loose descriptions; as patterns mature, "crystallize" them into formal types.
- **Borrowable Asset:** **"Gradual Formalization."** Never lock a schema until the "Residue Reingestion" frequency justifies it.

### D. Research Lab Notebooks
- **Key Concepts:** Process Memory, Lab Notes as "Experimental Trace."
- **External View:** The "Notebook" is a temporal record of *failures* as much as *facts*.
- **Borrowable Asset:** **"Failure Traces."** Recording why something *didn't* work is as important as recording what *did*.

### E. Tools for Thought (Non-linear)
- **Key Concepts:** Evergreen Notes (Matuschak), Zettelkasten.
- **External View:** Atomic notes prevent "Structural Collapse" by keeping pieces independent.
- **Borrowable Asset:** **"Atomic Residue."** Keeping reingest candidates small and independent to allow future re-aggregation.

- **Entry Chaos:** Is not a "disorder" but a **"High-Entropy Maturation Phase."** External observers mistake high entropy for lack of structure.
- **Productive Ambiguity:** Our lack of immediate schema allows the system to support "Multiple Lenses" simultaneously, whereas a fixed schema would force all lenses into one view.

## 6. External Critique

### Strengths
- **Interpretive Flexibility:** Because we don't lock schemas, our residue can be "re-read" through any lens (Layer-Awareness).
- **Ontological Resilience:** We survive "Paradigm Shifts" better than schema-first tools because we have no schema to break.

### Weaknesses
- **Communication Deficit:** We lack the "Ambient Status Indicators" that external systems use to signal "Space State" to observers (e.g., a "Stable" vs. "Experimental" layer).

### Misunderstanding Risk
- **"Just unorganized notes":** Users expect "folders" or "tags." We need a "Layer-Visualizer" that shows the space is maturing, even if it has no folders.

### Differentiation
- We treat **Ambiguity as a Design Resource**, whereas others treat it as a **Bug to be Fixed**.

### Borrowable Assets
- **"Gradual Formalization":** A policy of only formalizing patterns after $N$ occurrences (e.g., from `residue` to `pattern_candidate`).
- **"Boundary Objects":** Storing residues as objects that support multiple "Lens Reading" projections.
- **"Failure Traces":** Explicitly logging *why* an implementation path was abandoned to save future compute/attention.

### Do-not-borrow Assumptions
- **"Ambiguity is always good":** Over-prolonging ambiguity creates "Cognitive Drag" and makes the space impossible to navigate.
- **"Users will naturally form structure":** Without "Ambient Indicators," users feel lost in the entropy.
- **"Reverse Ontology solves everything":** It solves rigidity but creates a "Discoverability" problem.
